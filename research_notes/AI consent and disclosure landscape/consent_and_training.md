## 1. Who has explicitly framed the AI-art objection as about consent (the "3 Cs": consent, credit, compensation)?

### Takeaway
The "consent / credit / compensation" framing is well established and predates Nathan's talk by about three years. Visual artists started it in 2022–23 (Karla Ortiz, the Concept Art Association, Molly Crabapple's CAIR letter). Musicians (Artist Rights Alliance, 2024) and a cross-industry coalition (Ed Newton-Rex's Statement on AI Training, Oct 2024, later 30k+ signatories) took it up. Fairly Trained (2024) and the UK "Make It Fair" and silent-album campaigns (2025) turned it into institutions. Nathan's point 1 ("the ick is about consent") is mainstream within the creator movement. It is not a new claim.

### Cited Findings
- **Karla Ortiz (illustrator, MTG and Marvel concept artist).** She testified to the US Senate Judiciary IP Subcommittee on 12 July 2023. She said AI developers train on copyrighted work by human creators without asking, crediting or paying them, and called the systems wholly dependent on "vast quantities of copyrighted work made by human creators" (paraphrase from the testimony PDF summary; verify exact wording) — [Senate testimony PDF](https://www.judiciary.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/2023-07-12_pm_-_testimony_-_ortiz.pdf)
- Ortiz on NPR (29 Jul 2023): she found her whole body of fine art in the LAION dataset and said this took away her ability to consent. She is open to licensing at fair rates but rejects "a couple of cents" while companies earn millions (paraphrase) — [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2023/07/29/1190910319/an-artist-explains-why-marvels-use-of-ai-to-animate-a-sequence-is-worrying)
- Ortiz is a named plaintiff, with Sarah Andersen and Kelly McKernan, in **Andersen v. Stability AI** (filed Jan 2023, N.D. Cal., Judge Orrick; defendants Stability, Midjourney, DeviantArt, later Runway). Status as of early 2026: discovery is ongoing and the schedule was pushed back at plaintiffs' request. The summary-judgment hearing moved from 4 Nov 2026 to **17 Feb 2027**. An earlier Sept 2026 trial date appears superseded [secondary, verify on docket] — [ChatGPTiseatingtheworld, 3 Feb 2026](https://chatgptiseatingtheworld.com/2026/02/03/sarah-andersens-copyright-lawsuit-the-1st-filed-v-ai-companies-gets-pushed-back-again-at-her-request/); [status report, 14 Jan 2026](https://chatgptiseatingtheworld.com/2026/01/14/sarah-andersen-v-stability-ai-filed-status-report-to-judge-orrick-ahead-of-jan-20-conference/)
- **Molly Crabapple and the Center for Artistic Inquiry and Reporting (CAIR)**, May 2023. Their open letter asks publishers and newsrooms not to use AI-generated illustration. It objects to datasets of copyrighted images taken "without an artist's knowledge or consent" and asks outlets to stand against "corporate theft." Crabapple's statement: "Art corporations are poised to destroy illustrators' livelihoods, and they're using illustrators' own stolen images to do it" (as quoted by Artnet). Signatures grew from about 970 to over 4,000 — [Artnet](https://news.artnet.com/art-world/open-letter-urges-publishers-not-to-use-ai-generated-illustrations-2294392); [Hypebae](https://hypebae.com/2023/5/molly-crabapple-artificial-intelligence-art-open-petition-details). Crabapple later hosted the CAIR podcast "Pretty Vacant" on AI and art — [mollycrabapple.com](https://www.mollycrabapple.com/news/molly-crabapple-hosts-prettty-vacant-podcast-brian-merchant)
- **Ed Newton-Rex** resigned as VP of Audio at Stability AI in Nov 2023 over the company's fair-use position. He wrote the one-sentence **Statement on AI Training** (launched 22 Oct 2024), which calls unlicensed use of creative works for training a "major, unjust threat" to creators' livelihoods and says it "must not be permitted" (wording via secondary sources; confirm against aitrainingstatement.org). About 10,500 signatures at launch, 13,500 within days, and 31,000+ later per the British Copyright Council. Signatories include Thom Yorke, Björn Ulvaeus, Julianne Moore, Kevin Bacon, Kazuo Ishiguro, Robert Smith, SAG-AFTRA, AFM, UMG and the International Publishers Association — [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/computing/artificial-intelligence/kevin-bacon-julianne-moore-thom-yorke-and-10k-creators-sign-warning-against-ai-use-of-their-work); [Publishing Perspectives, Oct 2024](https://publishingperspectives.com/2024/10/uk-us-publishers-associations-join-international-ai-statement/); [British Copyright Council](https://www.britishcopyright.org/bcc-signs-global-ai-statement)
- Newton-Rex has also objected to the term "training data" as dehumanizing creators' work. Per the same coverage, he argues AI firms spend heavily on people and compute but expect data for free [secondary] — [Mixmag](https://mixmag.net/amp/10-000-creatives-ai-warning-statement). He has a book, *The Intelligence Heist* (Simon & Schuster UK) — [S&S listing](https://www.simonandschuster.co.uk/books/The-Intelligence-Heist/Ed-Newton-Rex/9781398553767)
- **Fairly Trained** (non-profit, launched Jan 2024, founded by Newton-Rex). Its "Licensed Model" certification is for companies that get permission for copyrighted training data. It will not certify companies relying on fair use. The scope was expanded to LLMs and voice, and in Aug 2025 the standards were tightened: certified firms must tell users which parts of their architecture are and are not certified. The Authors Guild endorsed it — [Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/music-ai-platforms-can-get-a-fairly-trained-certification-for-meeting-ethical-standards-those-standards-just-got-tougher/); [Authors Guild](https://authorsguild.org/news/ag-supports-fairly-trained-ai-licensing-model/); [Fairly Trained blog](https://www.fairlytrained.org/blog)
- The **Concept Art Association** (with Ortiz among its organizers) ran a 2022–23 crowdfunded campaign to hire a DC lobbyist on AI and artists' rights. I found no fresh source this session, so treat any specific wording as [verify].

### Inferences
- The consent framing is the movement's own vocabulary, so Nathan should present it as building on Ortiz, Crabapple and Newton-Rex, not as a discovery. What he can add is (a) the "influence follows reach" reciprocity mechanism and (b) the source/audience/author three-consent split. The creator movement mostly bundles these together as consent + credit + compensation.
- Ortiz's own position allows for licensing ("fair compensation"). The mainstream consent critique is therefore mostly "ask and pay", not "never train." Nathan's framing fits that.

### Gaps
- I could not verify who first coined "consent, credit, compensation" as a slogan. It appears across artist advocacy in 2022–23 (Ortiz, CAA, the Authors Guild and the Human Artistry Campaign use variants). The exact origin is unconfirmed.
- I could not fetch the live signature count or exact text at aitrainingstatement.org.
- I found no current count of Fairly Trained–certified models.

## 2. Ted Chiang: what he actually argues (intention, choices) and how it relates to consent vs. authenticity

### Takeaway
Chiang's 2024 New Yorker essay argues that art comes from many choices and from intention. Generative AI dilutes intention, so it cannot make art and its text is not communication. This is an **authenticity / authorship** argument, not a consent argument. It maps onto Nathan's "author consent" (byline as promise) and "audience consent," not onto "source consent." "Blurry JPEG" (2023) is about lossy compression and reliability, also not about consent.

### Cited Findings
- "Why A.I. Isn't Going to Make Art," *The New Yorker*, published 31 Aug 2024 (print issue Sept 2024) [bibliographic, not re-fetched; newyorker.com blocked]. Per commentators, the core argument is that art results from making many choices. A 10,000-word story involves on the order of 10,000 choices, while a short prompt involves few, and the model fills in the rest with averages. He uses photography as the counterexample: the medium seemed choice-free at first, but its artistry lies in the photographer's many choices — [Countercraft, "Art Without Intention"](https://countercraft.substack.com/p/art-without-intention); [Kai Peschl](https://kaipeschl.substack.com/p/ai-and-art-my-thoughts-on-ted-chiangs)
- In a March 2025 Princeton lecture expanding the essay, Chiang said: "If we think of art as a concentrated form of intention, then generative AI is a way of diluting intention" — [Princeton AI Lab news, 2025](https://ai.princeton.edu/news/2025/ted-chiang-explores-%E2%80%9Cincompatibilities%E2%80%9D-between-generative-ai-and-art)
- The essay also argues that LLM output is not communication because there is no intention to communicate behind it. I recall this from the essay but could not verify exact wording, so paraphrase only [verify].
- Critics say the essay spends much of its length rebutting assorted pro-AI arguments rather than theorizing art, and that the "choices" test would not survive critical theory even if it matches folk intuitions — [Countercraft](https://countercraft.substack.com/p/art-without-intention); [Last Week's New Yorker Review](https://buttondown.com/lastweeksnewyorker/archive/last-weeks-new-yorker-review-the-weekend-special-5741/)
- "ChatGPT Is a Blurry JPEG of the Web," *The New Yorker*, 9 Feb 2023 [bibliographic, not re-fetched]. LLMs are compared to lossy compression of the web; output is a paraphrase that looks like understanding; and training models on their own output degrades them further. The concern is reliability and value, not consent. Chiang's other 2023 piece, "Will A.I. Become the New McKinsey?" (New Yorker, May 2023), is about AI concentrating capital and power [bibliographic, not re-fetched].

### Inferences
- Chiang is the best-known voice for the **authenticity** objection, and this is analytically separate from the **consent** objection. A model trained entirely on licensed, consented data would still fail Chiang's test. A human ghost-writer would pass it but could still violate Nathan's "author consent / byline as promise." Nathan can use Chiang to show that the "ick" has at least two components and to argue that consent is the one that generalizes across media.
- Nathan's "author consent" (the byline as a promise about who made the choices) is close to Chiang's view: if a byline claims choices the named author did not make, it misrepresents the work.

### Gaps
- Exact quotes from both New Yorker essays could not be checked (site blocked). Paraphrase, or check the originals before quoting.
- I did not find a statement where Chiang addresses training-data consent directly.

## 3. "Influence follows reach": has anyone articulated the implicit social contract of influence and reciprocity?

### Takeaway
The general idea that AI breaks an implicit **reciprocity / social contract** of sharing is well articulated, most prominently by **Creative Commons** ("Reciprocity in the Age of AI," Apr 2025; CC signals billed as "a new social contract for the age of AI," Jun 2025). Data-commons researchers (Longpre et al., Open Future) and Lanier's data dignity also cover it. Kirby Ferguson's "Everything is a Remix" supplies the copy/transform/combine account of creativity that both sides use. I found **no one** using Nathan's precise formulation: influence was historically gated by **reach** (you could only be copied if you were heard, and being heard brought recognition back), while AI takes from obscure and famous alike, returns no audience, and competes with the source. That specific mechanism appears to be Nathan's own contribution, but treat this as "not found", which is weaker than "doesn't exist."

### Cited Findings
- **Creative Commons, "Reciprocity in the Age of AI"** (2 Apr 2025): it warns that when entitlement leads to misuse, "the social contract breaks, reciprocation evaporates, and ultimately the magic weakens" (wording via search summary; verify). It proposes credit/attribution "when possible" and support for open infrastructure as forms of reciprocity [secondary; CC site not fetchable] — [CC blog](https://creativecommons.org/2025/04/02/reciprocity-in-the-age-of-ai/)
- CC's CEO (Anna Tumadóttir) calls attribution the "core tenet" of the commons and says LLMs often strip references to original creators — [World Economic Forum, Oct 2025](https://www.weforum.org/stories/2025/10/what-is-creative-commons-and-how-is-ai-challenging-it/)
- CC signals was announced in June 2025 under the framing "a new social contract for the age of AI," with the goals of "reciprocity, recognition, and sustainability" — [TechCrunch, 25 Jun 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/06/25/creative-commons-debuts-cc-signals-a-framework-for-an-open-ai-ecosystem); [The Living Library repost](https://thelivinglib.org/?p=60747). Details are in section 6.
- Justine Tunney, "AI Training Shouldn't Erase Authorship": training pipelines strip author metadata, and webmasters tightening robots.txt is a sign that trust in sharing has eroded — [justine.lol](https://justine.lol/history/)
- Open Future (EU think tank) argues that removing any individual work has no discernible effect on a model, so individual opt-out cannot deliver reciprocity. It proposes opt-outs plus a levy on commercial AI deployment as a collective return to the commons — [Open Future, "AI, the Commons, and the limits of copyright"](https://openfuture.eu/blog/ai-the-commons-and-the-limits-of-copyright/)
- **Kirby Ferguson, "Everything is a Remix"** (2010–12; remastered 2015; Part 2 / AI material ~2023): creativity is copy, transform, combine, and copyright wrongly assumes creativity is mostly original. Commentators use the remix thesis both to defend AI ("it's just remixing") and to question it. I found **no direct Ferguson statement on AI credit or attribution** — [Techdirt](https://www.techdirt.com/tag/art/); [UCT Ethics Lab, "Generative AI: Remix or Slop?", 4 May 2026](https://health.uct.ac.za/ethics-lab/articles/2026-05-04-generative-ai-remix-or-slop); [Human Risk podcast interview, Aug 2024](https://humanriskpodcast.com/kirby-ferguson-on-why-everything-is-a-remix)
- **Holly Herndon & Mat Dryhurst** are the main artist-practitioners of consent-first AI:
  - **Spawning** (co-founded 2022) builds consent infrastructure: haveibeentrained.com (search LAION for your work), the **Do Not Train registry** (one profile credits it with covering over 2 billion works [single source, verify]), Kudurru (scraper blocking) and Source.Plus (a dataset of public-domain and CC0 works) — [Mat Dryhurst, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mat_Dryhurst); [NoiseDAO, "Consensual data in the age…"](https://noisedao.substack.com/p/issue-10-consensual-data-in-the-age)
  - **Holly+** (2021) is a voice model of Herndon that the public can use, framed as open-sourcing a voice with shared, decentralized permissions — [Sounding Future](https://soundingfuture.com/en/article/holly-holly-herndons-digital-ai-voice-twin)
  - The earlier "Spawn" model (album *PROTO*, 2019) was trained on a paid, consenting Berlin vocal ensemble. This is an early worked example of consented training data — [same](https://soundingfuture.com/en/article/holly-holly-herndons-digital-ai-voice-twin)
  - Note that Herndon and Dryhurst are **pro-AI and pro-consent**. Their view is that permission and protocol, not abstinence, are the fix, and that is a useful contrast to the anti-AI framing.
- The homage-vs-theft norm in music is widely discussed in musicology (e.g., the "Blurred Lines" debate). I did not find a new 2023–26 scholarly piece applying it to AI that I could cite.

### Inferences
- The closest existing statement of Nathan's idea is CC's "the social contract breaks, reciprocation evaporates." CC frames reciprocity as **giving back to the commons**, while Nathan frames it as **recognition and audience flowing back to the individual source** in proportion to reach. That difference is the distinctive part and worth stating explicitly.
- Nathan's point that AI "takes from obscure and famous alike" is a sharp observation. Before AI, an obscure artist was rarely copied at scale because nobody saw their work. Training ingests the long tail indiscriminately. Data-provenance work (section 7) supports this empirically, since LAION and C4 scraped the long tail.

### Gaps
- No source found that uses "influence follows reach" or an equivalent reach-gated reciprocity framing.
- No direct Kirby Ferguson quote on AI found.
- How the Do Not Train registry works, who maintains it, and whether AI firms honor it in 2026 is unverified. (Stability AI and Hugging Face honored Spawning opt-outs in 2022–23, but that is from memory [verify].)

## 4. Jaron Lanier: data dignity / data-as-labor, and 2024–2026 updates

### Takeaway
Lanier (with E. Glen Weyl) has argued since about 2018 that data contributors should be recognized and paid ("data dignity," "data as labor"). His 2023 New Yorker essay "There Is No A.I." applied this to LLMs: treat models as social collaborations and track **provenance** so contributors can be credited and paid. I found no substantial new Lanier essay on this from 2025–26, only talks restating it. This is the strongest existing intellectual precedent for Nathan's "credit should flow back" claim and for provenance tooling like SlopChop.

### Cited Findings
- Core sources [bibliographic, not re-fetched]: Lanier & Weyl, "A Blueprint for a Better Digital Society," *Harvard Business Review*, 26 Sep 2018; Arrieta-Ibarra, Goff, Jiménez-Hernández, Lanier & Weyl, "Should We Treat Data as Labor? Moving Beyond 'Free'," *AEA Papers & Proceedings* 108 (May 2018); Posner & Weyl, *Radical Markets* (2018), ch. 5 "Data as Labor"; Lanier, *Who Owns the Future?* (2013).
- Lanier, "There Is No A.I.," *The New Yorker*, 20 Apr 2023 [bibliographic]. A Berkeley CDSS talk abstract describes it as reframing large models as "a social collaboration" between the people who provided data, with explanations of model behavior centered on the relative influence of inputs through a "provenance calculation" — [Berkeley CDSS, "Data Dignity and Inversion AI"](https://cdss.berkeley.edu/video/data-dignity-and-inversion-ai-jaron-lanier)
- TechCrunch (21 Apr 2023): even data-dignity researchers disagree on how to disentangle what a model absorbed and how fine-grained the accounting should be, though Lanier thinks it could be done "gradually" — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/04/21/as-ai-eliminates-jobs-a-way-to-keep-people-afloat-financially-thats-not-ubi)
- A related empirical result: an MIT study found that tracing a generated image back to specific training artists is hard. James Grimmelmann notes that without attribution, legal cases don't resolve neatly either way — [SAN](https://san.com/cc/if-your-art-trained-the-ai-mit-says-you-may-never-be-able-to-prove-it/)

### Inferences
- Lanier gives Nathan intellectual cover for "credit should flow back," and the technical-feasibility debate (training-data attribution is hard) is the strongest counterargument. SlopChop works on *copy provenance* of outputs, not *training-data attribution*, and Nathan should keep the two distinct.

### Gaps
- No verified 2024–26 Lanier publication updating data dignity was found. A Spanish 2026 blog mentions a "MID" proposal, but it is a single unreliable source.

## 5. Code: Doe v. GitHub, Stack Overflow, open-source positions, developer attitudes

### Takeaway
On the code side, the consent-was-conditional argument is being litigated mainly as **breach of open-source license / contract**, not copyright. In **Doe v. GitHub** the DMCA §1202(b) "stripped attribution" theory has now lost at the Ninth Circuit (16 Sep 2026). The license-contract claims survive in district court, and that directly tests Nathan's "MIT attribution / GPL share-alike" point. Stack Overflow's 2024 OpenAI deal caused a consent revolt that the company suppressed under its CC BY-SA/ToS terms, and SO question volume has since collapsed to near-launch levels. FSF and SFC objected to Copilot from 2021–22, and OSI's 2024 Open Source AI Definition was criticized for not requiring open training data.

### Cited Findings
**Doe v. GitHub (N.D. Cal. No. 4:22-cv-06823-JST; 9th Cir. No. 24-7700)**
- Filed Nov 2022 by Matthew Butterick and the Joseph Saveri Law Firm against GitHub, Microsoft and OpenAI. Notably, it did **not** plead direct copyright infringement. The theories were DMCA §1202 (removal of copyright management information), breach of open-source licenses, and various state-law claims [from memory; confirm against complaint] — [Knowing Machines explainer](https://knowingmachines.org/knowing-legal-machines/legal-explainer/cases/doe-1-v-github)
- In Jan 2024 Judge Tigar dismissed the DMCA CMI claims with leave to amend — [ChatGPTiseatingtheworld, 22 Jan 2024](https://chatgptiseatingtheworld.com/2024/01/22/judge-tigar-dismissses-dmca-cmi-claims-v-github-pdf-order/). On **24 June 2024** (unsealed later), the amended §1202(b) claims were dismissed for failing to plead "identicality" between output and the original. Unjust enrichment, tortious interference, fraud and similar claims were also dismissed, but the **breach-of-contract (open-source license) claims survived**. The court found an open-source agreement governing GitHub sharing was adequately alleged — [Mealey's](https://mealeys.com/mealeys/articles/2279479); [LWN](https://lwn.net/Articles/987524); [CO/AI](https://getcoai.com/news/judge-dismisses-most-claims-in-github-copilot-copyright-lawsuit-narrows-case)
- The court certified the §1202(b) identicality question for interlocutory appeal (granted 27 Sep 2024 [year inferred]), and the Ninth Circuit accepted it — [Mealey's](https://mealeys.com/mealeys/articles/2279479)
- **Ninth Circuit decision, 16 Sep 2026: affirmed dismissal.** The panel held that §1202(b) covers removing or altering CMI on an *existing* work. Copilot as alleged produces "new works that never contained that information," so nothing was removed. Plaintiffs had standing. A training-stage theory (that stripping notices before training was itself a violation) was held **forfeited**. The court expressly did **not** decide whether training on copyrighted code is lawful or whether near-verbatim outputs infringe. The breach-of-contract claims remain pending in district court — [Authors Alliance, 23 Sep 2026](https://www.authorsalliance.org/2026/09/23/resolving-an-interlocutory-appeal-ninth-circuit-affirms-dismissal-of-section-1202-dmca-claims-in-ongoing-doe-v-github-litigation/); [Fenwick](https://www.fenwick.com/insights/publications/nothing-remove-ninth-circuit-holds-copilot-creates-new-code-not-stripped); [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/new-code-old-rules-9th-circuit-says-github-copilots-output-not-copy-under-dmca); [Haynes Boone](https://www.haynesboone.com/news/alerts/ai-legal-news-ninth-circuit-rejects-dmca-section-1202(b)-theory); [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=e818f731-9009-442b-a348-4adbcea597a3) [all via search summaries; primary pages not fetchable here]

**Stack Overflow**
- On 6 May 2024 Stack Overflow and OpenAI announced an API partnership (OverflowAPI) giving OpenAI access to SO content, with attribution back to SO in ChatGPT. Contributors objected that they had not consented. Some edited or deleted their top answers in protest. Moderators reverted the edits and issued suspensions; one user ("Ben") reported a 7-day suspension within an hour. SO's ToS grants it an irrevocable licence to contributions, and content is CC BY-SA. The scale of the bans was not confirmed by SO — [The Register, 9 May 2024](https://www.theregister.com/2024/05/09/stack_overflow_banning_users_who/); [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/pro/stack-overflow-bans-users-for-altering-answers-to-protest-openai-deal); [ITPro](https://itpro.com/software/development/stack-overflow-hailed-its-openai-partnership-as-a-boon-for-developers-but-now-disgruntled-users-say-theyre-being-banned-for-deleting-posts)
- Context [from memory, verify]: SO signed a similar deal with Google (Feb 2024). In 2023 SO's moderators struck over the company's AI-content policy, and SO CEO Prashanth Chandrasekar framed licensing as "socially responsible AI" with attribution as a requirement.
- **Traffic and volume collapse.** Monthly questions peaked around 2014 at roughly 200k. One analysis puts the 2025 total at about 109k, against about 399k in 2024. June 2026 had about 1,072 questions, the lowest full month since the 2008 beta. July 2026 is reported as 1,304 or 1,442 depending on source. Exact figures vary by outlet and should be checked against the Stack Exchange Data Explorer. Some analysts note the decline began years before ChatGPT (moderation culture post-2014) but sharply accelerated after Nov 2022 — [Pragmatic Engineer](https://blog.pragmaticengineer.com/stack-overflow-is-almost-dead); [Slashdot, Jan 2026](https://tech.slashdot.org/story/26/01/05/1431212/); [Relevant Audience](https://www.relevantaudience.com/content-marketing/stack-overflow-1442-questions-july-2026/); [developersdigest](https://www.developersdigest.tech/blog/stackoverflow-ai-decline-graph-2026)
- **Stack Overflow Developer Survey 2024** (released Jul 2024): top AI ethical concerns were misinformation (79%), **"missing or incorrect attribution for sources of data" (65%)**, and bias (50%) — [Prosus summary, 25 Jul 2024](https://www.prosus.com/news-insights/2024/stack-overflow-2024-developer-survey); [SD Times](https://sdtimes.com/softwaredev/stack-overflow-developers-trust-in-ai-outputs-is-worsening-year-over-year/). In the 2025 survey (~49k respondents), 46% distrust AI output accuracy, up from 31% [secondary] — [CFOtech](https://cfotech.com.au/story/developer-trust-in-ai-tools-declines-despite-increased-usage)

**Open-source institutions**
- **FSF (Jul 2021)** called Copilot "unacceptable and unjust, from our perspective," mainly because it requires non-free software. It funded white papers on whether training on public repos infringes, whether it is fair use, and Copilot's risk of GPL violations — [InfoWorld](https://www.infoworld.com/article/3627319/github-copilot-is-unacceptable-and-unjust-says-free-software-foundation.html); [FSF blog](https://www.fsf.org/blogs/licensing/fsf-funded-call-for-white-papers-on-philosophical-and-legal-questions-around-copilot)
- **Software Freedom Conservancy.** Bradley Kuhn's "If Software is My Copilot, Who Programmed My Software?" (Feb 2022) proposes keeping training data, models and outputs all under copyleft — [SFC blog](https://sfconservancy.org/blog/2022/feb/03/github-copilot-copyleft-gpl/). The **"Give Up GitHub"** campaign (Jun 2022) urged projects to leave GitHub, citing GitHub's refusal to engage with the ethics of training Copilot on FOSS code — [desdelinux summary](https://blog.desdelinux.net/en/software-freedom-conservancy-urges-open-source-projects-to-stop-using-github/)
- **OSI Open Source AI Definition 1.0** (released 28 Oct 2024) requires "data information" (enough detail for a skilled person to build a "substantially equivalent" system), not release of the training data itself. Critics include SFC's Kuhn, who called the process acrimonious and the definition flawed for not requiring open data; Tom Callaway (AWS); RedMonk's Stephen O'Grady (the term shouldn't extend to AI at all); and LWN's Joe Brockmeier (the bar was set too low). Defenders include Mozilla's Ayah Bdeir and ex-OSI chair Carlo Piana — [The New Stack, "The Case Against OSI's Open Source AI Definition"](https://thenewstack.io/the-case-against-osis-open-source-ai-definition/); [The New Stack, release](https://thenewstack.io/the-open-source-ai-definition-is-out/); [LWN](https://lwn.net/Articles/995159/)
- **Developer attitudes, research.** In the BigCode/The Stack governance study, interviewed developers were mostly neutral-to-positive about their *permissively licensed* code being used for training, and none interviewed wanted to opt out. Among those who did request removal, reasons included preferring opt-in over opt-out and feeling uncompensated use was unfair — [BigCode Governance Card, arXiv 2312.03872](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.03872). A 2025 paper, "What Makes Code Generation Ethically Sourced?", notes opt-in consent is hard because mined repos often lack contact details, and suggests non-AI licences and consent-management systems — [arXiv 2507.19743](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.19743)

**Writing (for completeness)**
- **Bartz v. Anthropic**: Judge Alsup (June 2025) ruled that training on lawfully acquired books was fair use, but that acquiring and keeping 7M+ pirated books was not protected. A **$1.5B class settlement** (~$3,000/work, ~465k–482k works; sources differ) received final approval on **20 Jul 2026** from Judge Martínez-Olguín (Alsup retired end-2025). The release covers past acquisition and copying through 25 Aug 2025; output claims and future conduct are not released — [Authors Guild](https://authorsguild.org/news/court-grants-final-approval-anthropic-copyright-settlement/); [Pearl Cohen](https://pearlcohen.com/federal-court-approves-1-5-billion-anthropic-copyright-settlement-largest-in-history); [BetaNews](https://betanews.com/article/anthropic-copyright-settlement-approved/); [Publishers Lunch](https://lunch.publishersmarketplace.com/2026/07/anthropic-settlement-approved-attorney-fees-slashed)

### Inferences
- The Doe v. GitHub path backs Nathan's "conditional consent" framing. Courts have so far rejected attribution-stripping under the DMCA (output is "new"), leaving the **license-as-contract** theory as the live vehicle for enforcing MIT/GPL conditions. How that contract claim resolves is the key thing to watch.
- Stack Overflow is the clearest case of consent being "stretched": CC BY-SA plus the ToS gave the platform legal authority, contributors felt that was not what they agreed to, and the platform overrode their attempt to withdraw. The 65% attribution concern in SO's own survey shows that developers care about the *credit* condition specifically.
- The BigCode finding (permissive-license developers were largely fine with training) supports Nathan's spectrum. Code was the community where the original contract was stretched least, though the conditions (attribution, share-alike) are what got dropped.
- Bartz shows that US courts may treat training itself as fair use while punishing *how data was obtained*. Legally, "consent" is being enforced at the acquisition stage, not the training stage.

### Gaps
- I did not confirm the Doe v. GitHub district-court schedule after the 9th Circuit mandate, or whether plaintiffs will seek rehearing or certiorari.
- I found no large representative survey of open-source maintainers specifically on consent to training (2024–26).
- I did not find FSF's final position on whether training on GPL code requires model or output copyleft. Its white papers (2022) were exploratory.

## 6. Creative Commons: stated positions, and whether CC licenses were understood as consent to training

### Takeaway
CC's long-standing position (2023 onward) is that training on CC-licensed works is often permitted where it is fair use or a TDM exception, and that CC licences cannot by themselves forbid uses that copyright doesn't restrict. CC has nonetheless acknowledged that this broke contributors' expectations, and in 2025 launched **CC signals**, a preference-signal framework built on reciprocity. As of Dec 2025 it was still "in the pilot stages." So CC itself concedes the gap between legal permission and social consent that Nathan describes.

### Cited Findings
- CC signals was announced 25 Jun 2025 as "a new social contract for the age of AI." Creators and dataset holders attach machine-readable yes/no signals on AI training plus conditions (reported as four categories: credit, direct contribution, ecosystem contribution, open). Enforceability may range from legally binding to normative. An alpha was planned for about Nov 2025 — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/06/25/creative-commons-debuts-cc-signals-a-framework-for-an-open-ai-ecosystem); [eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/news/creative-commons-signals/); [Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/?p=334780)
- CC's Dec 2025 year-in-review: "We're still in the pilot stages of this work" — [CC, "What we built together in 2025"](https://creativecommons.org/2025/12/19/what-we-built-together-in-2025/) [secondary quote via search]
- "Reciprocity in the Age of AI" (Apr 2025): see section 3. The social contract "breaks, reciprocation evaporates" — [CC](https://creativecommons.org/2025/04/02/reciprocity-in-the-age-of-ai/)
- Researchers note that even permissive licences usually require attribution, which LLM developers rarely satisfy — [KL3M Data Project, arXiv 2504.07854](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.07854)
- CC's earlier view (2021–23 FAQ and blog: "Understanding CC Licenses and Generative AI," Aug 2023) is that if training is fair use or excepted, the licence conditions, including attribution, may not apply, and CC licences were not drafted to address AI [bibliographic/memory, verify].

### Inferences
- This is direct institutional support for Nathan's point 3 ("consent was conditional and stretched"). Attribution was the defining condition of CC BY and CC BY-SA, and the licence steward itself says AI use may fall outside licence conditions altogether. CC's answer is a new signalling layer, not licence enforcement.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm whether the CC signals alpha launched or what its 2026 status is. The creativecommons.org site was unreachable from this environment.
- The exact current CC FAQ wording on licences and AI training is unverified.

## 7. Music: lawsuits and settlements, artist statements, consent and opt-in licensing

### Takeaway
Labels sued Suno and Udio in June 2024, and by late 2025 the fights were turning into **licensing deals**: UMG–Udio (Oct 2025), WMG–Suno and WMG–Udio (Nov 2025), with promises of models trained only on licensed catalogues. Sony and UMG remained in litigation against Suno into 2026, per secondary trackers. Critics call this "launch, train, settle," and musicians are now challenging labels for licensing members' work **without the artists' own consent**. That moves the consent question from AI firm vs. rightsholder to rightsholder vs. creator, a nuance Nathan's framing can use.

### Cited Findings
- **Artist Rights Alliance open letter "Stop Devaluing Music"** (2 Apr 2024), with 200+ signatories including Billie Eilish, Finneas, Nicki Minaj, Stevie Wonder, Smokey Robinson, Jon Bon Jovi, Elvis Costello, R.E.M., Sheryl Crow, and the Bob Marley and Frank Sinatra estates. It calls for an end to the "predatory use of AI" and says some companies are, without permission, using artists' work to train models. It does **not** call for a ban and acknowledges AI's potential when used responsibly — [MusicTech](https://musictech.com/news/industry/billie-eilish-jon-bon-jovi-open-letter-ai-music); [CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/1.7163901)
- **"Is This What We Want?"** (25 Feb 2025), a silent album of empty studios and performance spaces by 1,000+ UK musicians (Kate Bush, Annie Lennox, Cat Stevens, Damon Albarn, Imogen Heap, Hans Zimmer and others), organized by Ed Newton-Rex. It protested the UK proposal for a TDM exception with an **opt-out** for rightsholders, which critics said reverses copyright's default of consent. It ran alongside the "Make It Fair" newspaper campaign and a letter to The Times from 34 artists (incl. Dua Lipa, Elton John, Ed Sheeran, Sting) — [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/musicians-silent-album-protest-uk-proposed-ai-copyright-law-change-rcna193660); [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2025/2/25/music-stars-release-silent-album-in-protest-against-uk-ai-copyright-plans)
- **UK outcome.** The government's report under ss.135–136 of the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025, published **18 Mar 2026**, dropped the opt-out exception as its preferred approach without endorsing an alternative. Of ~11,500 consultation responses, roughly 81–88% preferred licensing-based options (sources differ) and about 3% backed opt-out. Next steps include more evidence gathering, a digital-replicas consultation, and a labelling taskforce due autumn 2026 — [Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/articles/uk-copyright-and-ai-report-the-opt-out-is-dead-but-what-comes-next/); [Osborne Clarke](https://www.osborneclarke.com/insights/opted-out-uk-government-moves-away-preferred-position-ai-and-copyright-following-report); [Holyrood](https://www.holyrood.com/news/view,ai-copyright-optout-scrapped-as-uk-government-seeks-more-time-for-reform)
- **Suno/Udio lawsuits** (filed Jun 2024 by RIAA labels in D. Mass. and S.D.N.Y.):
  - UMG–Udio settlement and partnership, **Oct 2025**, with a joint licensed platform planned for 2026 (reported as a walled garden without downloads). Forbes reports a royalty of $0.002–0.005 per generation, but terms were not officially disclosed — [Forbes, Virginie Berger, 18 Dec 2025, "Launch, Train, Settle"](https://www.forbes.com/sites/virginieberger/2025/12/18/launch-train-settle-how-suno-and-udios-licensing-deals-made-copyright-infringement-profitable/)
  - WMG–Suno settlement, **25 Nov 2025**: Suno to launch licensed models in 2026 and retire current ones, restrict downloads to paid users, and acquire Songkick from WMG — [Daily Star (Reuters-sourced)](https://www.thedailystar.net/entertainment/music/news/warner-music-settles-lawsuit-suno-forms-new-ai-partnership-4045651); [Digital Policy Alert](https://digitalpolicyalert.org/event/35757-warner-music-group-reached-settlement-of-litigation-and-formed-licensed-ai-partnership-agreement-with-suno-case-no-124-cv-11611)
  - WMG–Udio also settled (Nov 2025), per multiple reports, though one tracker disagrees [verify].
  - **UMG and Sony vs. Suno** were still being litigated in 2026, with dispositive motions reportedly due Apr 2027. Sony had not settled with Udio, and one source reports Sony filed a new suit against Udio in July 2026 [low-quality secondary; verify] — [happycapyguide](https://happycapyguide.com/blog/suno-umg-sony-licensing-stalemate-ai-music-copyright-2026); [Chartlex tracker](https://www.chartlex.com/blog/business/music-industry-ai-lawsuits-tracker-2026); [Dynamoi timeline](https://dynamoi.com/learn/ai-music-distribution/ai-music-copyright-cases-timeline)
  - **AFM (American Federation of Musicians) suit vs. Warner and Universal** alleges the labels licensed members' recordings to Suno and Udio without musicians' consent or payment [single secondary source; verify date and court] — [Time To House](https://timetohouse.com/en/articles/musicians-union-sues-warner-universal-ai-suno-udio)
- **Consent and opt-in models:** Fairly Trained certification (section 1); Holly+ and Spawning (section 3). Kits AI has had voice and instrument models certified by Fairly Trained — [MBW](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/music-ai-platforms-can-get-a-fairly-trained-certification-for-meeting-ethical-standards-those-standards-just-got-tougher/)

### Inferences
- The label settlements show consent obtained from **rightsholders**, not necessarily from **artists**, which is a fourth party in Nathan's model. ## 8. Academic work on "consent" in AI training data

### Takeaway
The canonical citation is **Longpre et al., "Consent in Crisis: The Rapid Decline of the AI Data Commons"** (Data Provenance Initiative; arXiv Jul 2024; NeurIPS 2024 Datasets & Benchmarks). It documents web publishers withdrawing consent through robots.txt and ToS at scale in 2023–24, and finds that web consent protocols were not designed for AI repurposing. It is direct empirical support for "the contract was stretched, and publishers are now pulling back."

### Cited Findings
- The paper audited about 14,000 web domains behind C4, RefinedWeb and Dolma, which the authors call the first large-scale longitudinal audit of consent protocols for AI training corpora. Over one year (2023–24), about **5%+ of all C4 tokens** and **~25–28%+ of tokens from the most critical, actively maintained sources** became fully restricted via robots.txt. Under Terms of Service, **~45% of C4** is now restricted. Websites' ToS and robots.txt are often inconsistent, which the authors attribute to protocols "not designed to cope with the widespread re-purposing of the internet for AI." If respected, the restrictions will bias the diversity, freshness and scaling of general-purpose AI — [arXiv 2407.14933](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.14933); [NeurIPS 2024 proceedings](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/c3738949a80306cc48a8ea8ba0560f9d-Paper-Datasets_and_Benchmarks_Track.pdf); [Open Future summary](https://openfuture.eu/note/consent-in-crisis-the-rapid-decline-of-the-ai-data-commons/)
- Open Future notes that the burden of restriction falls hardest on small firms and researchers who cannot afford licences. This is a "consent vs. openness" tension worth acknowledging — [Open Future](https://openfuture.eu/note/consent-in-crisis-the-rapid-decline-of-the-ai-data-commons/)
- Longpre et al., "Data Authenticity, Consent, & Provenance for AI are all broken: what will it take to fix them?" (2024) argues these three are interlinked infrastructure failures — [arXiv 2404.12691](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.12691). This is closely parallel to Nathan's three-consent and provenance framing.
- The paper was presented at the IETF AI-control workshop (2024), feeding standards work on AI preference signals — [IETF datatracker](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/slides-aicontrolws-consent-in-crisis-the-rapid-decline-of-the-ai-data-commons/00/)
- On the legal side, US copyright has weak moral rights, so there is no general federal right of attribution. This means "credit" has little legal footing outside licences — [JIPLP, "Attribution problem of generative AI"](https://academic.oup.com/jiplp/article/18/11/796/7271384)
- A global survey of AI researchers reportedly found only about 1 in 4 think AI firms should be allowed to train on any publicly available text or images [secondary via search; source unverified] — [UCL / LSE blog, O'Donovan 2025](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10220138/1/odonovan_2025_lse_blog.pdf)

### Inferences
- "Consent in Crisis" plus "Data Authenticity, Consent, & Provenance … are all broken" are the two citations that best anchor Nathan's thesis academically. The second explicitly links consent (source) and authenticity/provenance (audience and author), which is close to his three-consent structure. He should cite it to avoid appearing to claim originality for that linkage.
- On Nathan's three consents overall: **source consent** is heavily covered (everything above). **Audience consent** (disclosure, labelling) is covered by labelling and C2PA debates; the UK labelling taskforce and Fairly Trained's 2025 transparency rule are examples. **Author consent** (the byline as a promise) is the least developed under that name, though Chiang's intention argument and Longpre's "authenticity" pillar are adjacent.

### Gaps
- I did not check for a 2025–26 follow-up audit by the Data Provenance Initiative updating the robots.txt numbers.
- I found no academic work using the specific "audience consent" / "author consent" terminology. The adjacent literature uses "disclosure," "transparency," "authenticity," and "provenance."
