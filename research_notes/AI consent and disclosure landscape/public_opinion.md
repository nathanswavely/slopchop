# Public Opinion on AI (esp. AI in Creative Work), by Occupation, Politics, Age and Other Demographics — as of October 2026

> **Method note for the report writer:** The session's egress proxy blocked direct page fetches (pewresearch.org, gdconf.com, siepr.stanford.edu all returned connect_rejected). Every figure below comes from search-engine result text that summarized the linked page, cross-checked across 2+ outlets where possible. Figures marked **(secondary)** were seen only in third-party coverage. Exact wording and toplines should be spot-checked against the primary URL before you quote them on stage. Nothing here is invented. Where a number could not be found, it is listed under Gaps.

**What this means for the speaker's claims, in short:**
1. *"Occupation predicts AI stance better than politics"*: **partly supported, but it can't be tested directly.** No major US poll (Pew, Gallup, YouGov, AP-NORC, Ipsos) publishes AI attitudes broken down by creative vs. technical occupation. Indirect evidence fits the idea. Creative-trade surveys show heavy job losses and hostility. Gallup data show the partisan gap in AI *use* disappears once you control for occupation and education. But the GDC 2026 data cut against a clean "creative vs. technical" split: game *programmers* are nearly as negative as artists (59% vs 64%).
2. *"AI splits existing coalitions"*: **strongly supported for 2023–2025 and for elites.** The GOP is split (populist right vs. tech right) and 99 senators voted together against the moratorium. **But by mid/late 2026 it is re-sorting along party lines.** Democrats, especially liberals, are now clearly more negative than Republicans (Pew, YouGov, NBC, DFP).
3. *"No negotiated etiquette yet"*: **supported.** Large majorities want to know when content is AI-made, and a majority of workers hide their AI use. Experiments show a social penalty for AI users. No survey measures etiquette norms directly.
4. *Personas*: **broadly consistent with published segmentations.** The biggest group in every segmentation is an ambivalent middle ("Evaluators" 43%, Pew's "equally excited and concerned" 37%). The "Closeted User" persona is backed by multiple large studies (48–57% conceal use).

## 1. Pew Research Center: trends, partisanship, age, gender, creativity (2024–2026)

### Takeaway
Concern about AI is at a record high and still rising: 52% are more concerned than excited (June 2026), up from 37% in 2021. Young adults have flipped from the most enthusiastic group to the most wary. On partisanship, Pew shows a *reversal*: Republicans were more concerned in 2021–23, the parties were tied in 2025, and Democrats are now more concerned (2026). Pew finds the public expects AI to make people *worse* at creative thinking. Learning that art or music was made by AI turns about half of people off, and the young are the most turned off.

### Cited Findings
**Overall trend (Pew, survey June 22–28, 2026, n=3,488 US adults; published Aug 18, 2026)**
- 52% are more concerned than excited about the increased use of AI in daily life, 9% more excited than concerned, 37% equally both. This is up from 37% "more concerned" in 2021. — [Pew short read, Aug 18 2026](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2026/08/18/young-adults-in-the-us-are-increasingly-wary-of-ai-concerned-it-will-take-jobs/); [SSRS repost](https://ssrs.com/news/young-adults-concerned-about-ai/)
- 71% expect AI to lead to fewer US jobs over the next 20 years, up from 64% in 2024. Only 5% expect more jobs. — [Pew Aug 2026](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2026/08/18/young-adults-in-the-us-are-increasingly-wary-of-ai-concerned-it-will-take-jobs/); [AI Understanding summary](https://aiunderstanding.org/news/pew-survey-71-of-americans-expect-ai-to-reduce-u-s-jobs-over-20-years)
- **Under 30s:** 55% are now more concerned than excited, the first majority for this age group. That is up from 42% in 2023 and 31% in 2021. Only ~11% are more excited than concerned, down from 25% in 2021. 73% of under-30s expect fewer jobs, up from 61% in 2024. — [Pew Aug 2026](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2026/08/18/young-adults-in-the-us-are-increasingly-wary-of-ai-concerned-it-will-take-jobs/); [Courthouse News](https://www.courthousenews.com/pew-young-americans-grow-more-wary-of-ai-as-job-fears-rise/); [Bloomberg, Aug 19 2026](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-19/young-americans-become-more-hostile-to-ai-fearing-job-losses) (the 2021 and 2023 baselines come from different summaries; both may be correct)

**Partisanship: a reversal over time**
- 2021 and 2023: Republicans were about 15 points more likely than Democrats to be more concerned than excited. June 2025 (n=5,023, June 9–15, 2025): parity, with 50% of Republicans and 51% of Democrats. — [Pew, Nov 6 2025, "Republicans, Democrats now equally concerned about AI in daily life, but views on regulation differ"](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/11/06/republicans-democrats-now-equally-concerned-about-ai-in-daily-life-but-views-on-regulation-differ/)
- In the same 2025 data, regulation trust diverges: 54% of Republicans vs 36% of Democrats trust the US government to regulate AI effectively. 54% of Democrats trust the EU to do so. — [Pew Nov 2025](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/11/06/republicans-democrats-now-equally-concerned-about-ai-in-daily-life-but-views-on-regulation-differ/); [Tech Brew](https://www.techbrew.com/stories/2025/11/10/republicans-democrats-agree-ai-pew-survey)
- June 2026: 56% of Democrats vs 49% of Republicans are more concerned than excited. Democrats rose from 46% in 2023. Republicans fell 10 points since 2023. **Liberal Democrats: 63%, up from 45% in 2023. Conservative Republicans: down 14 points.** 75% of Democrats vs 68% of Republicans expect fewer jobs. — [Pew, Sept 16 2026, "Democrats now more worried than Republicans about AI and its impact on jobs"](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2026/09/16/democrats-are-now-more-worried-than-republicans-about-ai-and-its-impact-on-jobs/); [Benton](https://www.benton.org/headlines/democrats-are-now-more-worried-republicans-about-ai-and-its-impact-jobs); [FingerLakes1](https://www.fingerlakes1.com/2026/09/16/democrats-now-more-concerned-than-republicans-about-ai-and-jobs-pew-survey-finds/)
- 54% of Republicans vs 34% of Democrats say it is important for the US to be the AI leader. — [Pew, July 23 2026, "What Americans think about the global AI race"](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2026/07/23/what-americans-think-about-the-global-ai-race/) (secondary summary)
- June 2026 report: 67% have little or no confidence in the US government to regulate AI. Lack of confidence is 74% among Democrats vs 61% among Republicans. — [Pew "Americans and AI 2026" report PDF, June 17 2026](https://www.pewresearch.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/20/2026/06/PI_2026.06.17_Americans-and-AI_REPORT.pdf)

**Age and gender (Pew "Americans and AI 2026," survey Feb 17–23, 2026, 5,000+ adults; released June 17, 2026)**
- 49% of adults ever use chatbots, up from 33% in 2024. By age: 66% (18–29), 61% (30–49), 42% (50–64), 23% (65+). — [Pew, How opinions and use of AI differ by age](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2026/06/17/how-opinions-and-use-of-ai-differ-by-age/); [Pew chart](https://www.pewresearch.org/chart/adults-under-50-are-most-likely-to-use-chatbots-but-use-is-rising-across-age-groups/)
- **The youngest are the heaviest users AND the most negative.** Share expecting a negative impact on society: 48% (18–29), 39% (30–49), 37% (50+). Majorities in every age group (61–65%) say AI is advancing too fast. — [Pew by age](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2026/06/17/how-opinions-and-use-of-ai-differ-by-age/)
- Gender: chatbot use has nearly converged (50% of men vs 47% of women, vs 39%/28% in 2024; question wording changed). Daily use: 27% of men vs 20% of women. Women are more likely to say AI is moving too fast (68% vs 58%). "Chatbots help my productivity": 35% of men vs 25% of women. — [Pew, Oct 16 2026, "The gender gap in AI"](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2026/10/16/the-gender-gap-in-ai/); [Pew June 2026 gender chapter](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2026/06/17/the-gender-gap-in-ai/); [FingerLakes1](https://www.fingerlakes1.com/2026/10/17/pew-finds-women-more-skeptical-of-ai-despite-similar-chatbot-use/) (secondary)

**AI and creativity / creative works (Pew, survey June 9–15, 2025, n=5,023; published Sept 17, 2025)**
- 53% say AI will make people *worse* at thinking creatively. 16% say better, 16% neither. On problem-solving: 38% worse vs 29% better. — [Pew, "How Americans View AI and Its Impact on People and Society"](https://www.pewresearch.org/science/2025/09/17/how-americans-view-ai-and-its-impact-on-people-and-society/); [report PDF](https://www.pewresearch.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/20/2025/09/PS_2025.9.15_AI-and-its-impact_report.pdf)
- 76% say it is extremely or very important to be able to tell whether pictures, videos or text were made by AI or by people. 53% are not too or not at all confident they could tell. Only ~12% are extremely or very confident. — [Pew topline](https://alpha.pewresearch.org/pewresearch-org/wp-content/uploads/sites/20/2025/09/PS_2025.9.17_AI-and-its-impact_topline.pdf); [New Space Economy summary](https://newspaceeconomy.ca/2025/10/15/new-poll-what-do-americans-think-about-artificial-intelligence/?amp=1). Note: Search Engine Journal mischaracterized this as "76% want AI labels." It is an importance-of-knowing item, not a labeling item.
- Seven "after-the-fact" scenarios: if a painting they liked turned out to be AI-made, 49% would like it less and 48% would not change their view. Responses lean negative in all seven scenarios, most strongly for news and politics (56% would react negatively to learning an informative news article was AI-written). **By age: 66% of under-30s would like a painting less and 53% a song less, vs 36% and 26% of those 65+.** — [Pew short read, Sept 17 2025, "From political speeches to songs…"](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/09/17/from-political-speeches-to-songs-how-would-americans-react-if-they-found-out-ai-was-involved/) (age figures from secondary coverage; overall song figure inconsistent across summaries: ~one-third "like less" per one outlet)
- 2023 Pew: fewer than a third (31%) considered AI art a "major advance." — cited in [Frontiers in Psychology 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1509974/pdf) (secondary)

**Public vs AI experts (Pew, public survey Aug 12–18, 2024 and expert survey Aug–Oct 2024; published April 3, 2025)**
- 51% of the public vs 15% of experts are more concerned than excited. 56% of the public vs 25% of experts are extremely or very concerned about AI eliminating jobs. 64% of the public expects fewer jobs. The public is more likely than experts to expect job loss for musicians, teachers and doctors. — [Pew, "How the US Public and AI Experts View AI"](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2025/04/03/how-the-us-public-and-ai-experts-view-artificial-intelligence/); [report PDF](https://www.pewresearch.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/20/2025/04/pi_2025.04.03_us-public-and-ai-experts_report.pdf)

**Workers (Pew, Oct 7–13, 2024, n=5,273 employed adults; published Feb 25, 2025)**
- 52% of workers feel worried about future AI use at work, 36% hopeful, 33% overwhelmed, 29% excited. Only 6% expect more opportunities for themselves. 16% say at least some of their work is done with AI. Ages 18–49 are more excited than 50+ (32% vs 24%). Upper-income workers are more hopeful (45%) than lower-income (30%). — [Pew, "U.S. Workers Are More Worried Than Hopeful…"](https://www.pewresearch.org/social-trends/2025/02/25/u-s-workers-are-more-worried-than-hopeful-about-future-ai-use-in-the-workplace/)
- The only Pew industry cut found is from 2023: 32% of information/technology-sector workers said AI would help more than hurt them personally, vs 19% of all workers. — [Pew, July 26 2023](https://www.pewresearch.org/social-trends/2023/07/26/workers-views-on-the-risk-of-ai-to-their-jobs/)

### Inferences
- The speaker's "it's not politics" framing was most accurate around 2025 (Pew's 50/51 tie). By June 2026 a partisan gap had opened, with liberal Democrats up 18 points in three years. AI may be in the early stages of the familiar partisan sorting that many issues go through. The speaker should present this as a cleavage that is *currently being absorbed* into partisanship, not as one that is immune to it.
- The age finding is counterintuitive and useful on stage. Young adults are the biggest users AND the most hostile to AI art (66% would like a painting less). That fits the "Closeted User" and "Wary Pragmatist" personas better than the "Enthusiast" one.

### Gaps
- Pew does not publish attitudes by *occupation* (creative vs technical) in any 2024–2026 report found. The only industry cut is from 2023 (info/tech sector).
- Partisan breakdowns of the Pew creativity, painting and song items were not surfaced. They may exist in the topline/crosstabs.
- Under-30 attitudes by party were not found.

## 2. Other national polls: YouGov, Gallup, AP-NORC, NBC, Data for Progress, Ipsos, labeling

### Takeaway
Across pollsters, 2026 opinion is net-negative and worsening, and the party gap is widening. Democrats and independents moved sharply toward concern in Aug–Sept 2026 while Republicans held steady. Gender and age gaps are large. Women are consistently more negative. Age results depend on the question: young people are more *favorable* on generic favorability (DFP) but more *concerned* on impact questions (Pew, Gallup Gen Z). Support for labeling AI content is very high (71–89%) and appears bipartisan, though few labeling polls publish party splits.

### Inferences
- The DFP and Pew/Gallup age results only look contradictory. Young people *use* and *like the idea of* AI more, but are more pessimistic about its impact and more hostile to AI art. That ambivalence is exactly the speaker's Wary Pragmatist / Closeted User territory.
- The gender gap (women more negative across Pew, DFP, NBC) is a demographic cleavage the speaker's framing leaves out. It is at least as robust as any occupational effect we can document.

### Gaps
- No US national poll found that cross-tabs AI-art acceptance or labeling demand by occupation or industry.
- Party breakdowns of labeling-demand polls were not surfaced (Verasight, Fox, Pew 76% item).
- I found no AP-NORC poll specifically on AI in art, music or writing for 2025–2026.
- Ipsos US-specific 2026 figures were not retrieved.

## 3. Does AI cut across partisanship? (populist right + progressive left vs tech-right; AI as a new cleavage)

### Takeaway
At the *elite and coalition* level, yes. AI split MAGA (populists like Bannon, DeSantis, Hawley and Blackburn vs. Sacks and the tech right), and preemption of state AI laws failed 99–1 in the Senate. At the *mass-public* level, the 2025 tie has given way to a Democratic-leaning skeptic bloc in 2026. Still, the partisan gap on AI (~7 points on Pew's concern measure) is far smaller than on most issues. One rigorous paper finds the partisan gap in workplace AI *use* is explained by education, industry and occupation, which is indirect support for the speaker's occupational thesis.

### Cited Findings
- **Senate vote, July 1, 2025:** the Senate voted 99–1 to strip a 10-year moratorium on state AI regulation from the GOP reconciliation bill. The amendment was co-sponsored by Sen. Marsha Blackburn (R-TN) and Sen. Maria Cantwell (D-WA), after a failed Blackburn–Cruz 5-year compromise. — [Senate Commerce (Dem) press release](https://www.commerce.senate.gov/2025/7/senate-strikes-ai-moratorium-from-budget-reconciliation-bill-in-overwhelming-99-1-vote/); [States Newsroom via Florida Phoenix](https://floridaphoenix.com/?p=232700)
- Congress also rejected preemption language in the annual defense bill (NDAA). — [Axios, Dec 11 2025](https://axios.com/2025/12/11/trump-signs-executive-order-state-ai-laws)
- **Trump executive order, Dec 11, 2025,** targeting state AI laws: it created an AI Litigation Task Force at DOJ and conditioned some federal grants on compliance. Steve Bannon attacked David Sacks for having "face-planted twice on jamming AI Amnesty into must-pass legislation." Gov. Ron DeSantis said Florida would still regulate AI. Opposition came from "the populist left and the populist right… for largely different reasons." — [The Hill, "Trump picks fight with MAGA allies"](https://thehill.com/homenews/administration/5650327-trump-executive-order-ai/); [Axios, "MAGA scrambles to influence Trump's AI executive order"](https://www.axios.com/2025/12/11/maga-scrambles-trump-ai-executive-order); [EPI policy watch](https://www.epi.org/policywatch/executive-order-to-challenge-or-deter-state-laws-that-would-impact-artificial-intelligence-ai/)
- CNN (Feb 2, 2026): "Trump's AI push exposes a divide in the MAGA movement." — [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/02/02/politics/artificial-intelligence-maga-divide-trump); also [MacIver Institute, "A Federalism Fight Splits MAGA"](https://www.maciverinstitute.com/perspectives/a-federalism-fight-splits-maga)
- **Newsweek AI Policy Scorecard (2026):** the average Democratic member of Congress scores 2.60 ("Lean Skeptic") and the average Republican 3.26 ("Lean Booster") on a 1–5 scale. The 0.66-point gap is "relatively small." — [Newsweek, "How AI upended politics and left lawmakers scrambling to pick a side"](https://www.newsweek.com/ai-politics/how-ai-upended-politics-and-left-lawmakers-scrambling-to-pick-a-side-12501075)
- **Bloom & Makridis, "The Politics of AI"** (NBER WP 34813 / Stanford SIEPR, Feb 2026; Gallup Workforce Panel Q2 2024–Q1 2026): Democrats report more frequent AI use at work (Q4 2025: 27.8% weekly or daily vs 22.5% of Republicans). But the gap "disappears once we control for education, industry, and occupation." The authors call it "largely an artifact of educational and occupational sorting rather than ideological differences." — [NBER w34813](https://www.nber.org/papers/w34813); [PDF](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w34813/w34813.pdf); [SIEPR](https://siepr.stanford.edu/publications/working-paper/politics-ai)
- Mass-public partisan data (see §1–2): Pew shows R>D in 2021/23, parity in 2025 (50/51), D>R in 2026 (56/49; liberal Dems 63%). YouGov "very serious risk" in Sept 2026: D 53%, I 45%, R 29%. DFP: Republicans net +11 favorable, Democrats net unfavorable.
- A Johns Hopkins 2026 survey reportedly found Republicans and Democrats held broadly similar views on AI regulation. — via [Newsweek, "Democrats, GOP agree in new poll on major issue ahead of midterms—AI"](https://www.newsweek.com/democrats-gop-agree-in-new-poll-on-major-issue-ahead-of-midterms-ai-12413503) (secondary; details not retrieved)
- Commentary: a political-science Substack argues AI will politicize by *activating existing cleavages*. On this view the left frames AI as a labor and inequality issue and the right frames it as innovation and sovereignty. — [Kyle Saunders Substack](https://kylesaunders.substack.com/p/artificial-intelligence-and-politics) (opinion). Also [Washington Stand, "AI Is Scrambling America's Political Map"](https://washingtonstand.com/article/ai-is-scrambling-americas-political-map) (conservative outlet, commentary)

### Inferences
- The best-supported version of the speaker's claim: "AI cuts across *elite coalitions*: it split the GOP and united 99 senators. In the public, the parties were tied as recently as 2025." The claim "politics doesn't predict it" is now **overstated for late 2026**. Party ID and especially ideology (liberal vs conservative) now carry real signal, though the gaps are modest by US standards.
- The Republican softening (−10 points since 2023) coincides with the Trump administration's pro-AI posture. That pattern suggests partisan cue-taking, i.e., AI being *absorbed* into the existing cleavage rather than forming a new one. This is an inference, not a tested finding.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study was found that directly tests whether occupation (creative vs technical) predicts AI *attitudes* better than party ID. This is the speaker's core empirical claim, and **no published dataset found tests it.**
- I did not find good data on Hawley's role or on state-level vote breakdowns of AI bills (e.g., the Colorado/California fights).

## 4. Surveys of creative and technical professionals

### Takeaway
Creative-trade surveys show widespread income loss and deep hostility. A quarter to a half of illustrators, translators, photographers and novelists report lost work, and 85% of UK novelists expect lower future income. Game developers have turned sharply negative (52% say gen AI is hurting the industry, up from 18% two years earlier). That negativity reaches **programmers (59%)**, not just artists (64%). The real dividing line may be "makers of craft output vs managers/business roles" rather than "creative vs technical."

## 5. Hidden / "closeted" AI use and AI shame

### Takeaway
The Closeted User persona has strong support. Across very large samples, roughly half of workers conceal AI use or would be uncomfortable disclosing it (48–57%). A peer-reviewed PNAS study shows the fear is rational: observers really do rate AI users as lazier and less competent.

### Cited Findings
- **KPMG + University of Melbourne, "Trust, attitudes and use of AI: A global study 2025"** (48,000+ people in 47 countries, Nov 2024–Jan 2025): 57% of employees admit hiding their AI use at work and presenting AI-generated work as their own (Australia: 51%). Only 46% are willing to trust AI. Only 47% have received AI training. Researcher Nicole Gillespie links concealment to pressure to keep up and to employer bans. — [HCAMag](https://hcamag.com/au/specialisation/hr-technology/workers-admit-to-hiding-use-of-ai-in-their-work-according-to-major-new-global-report/533635); [Euronews, May 5 2025](https://www.euronews.com/next/2025/05/05/almost-half-of-workers-that-use-ai-on-the-job-dont-trust-it-new-survey-shows); [Melbourne Business School media](https://mbs.edu/faculty-and-research/trust-and-ai/media). (One MBS page cites "32,000+ workers," i.e., the employee subsample, vs 48,000 total.)
- **Microsoft & LinkedIn 2024 Work Trend Index** (May 2024; 31,000 knowledge workers in 31 countries): 75% use gen AI at work. 78% of AI users bring their own tools (BYOAI; 80% at SMBs). **52% of AI users are reluctant to admit using it for their most important tasks.** 53% worry that using it on important work makes them look replaceable. — [Microsoft WorkLab](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/worklab/work-trend-index/ai-at-work-is-here-now-comes-the-hard-part); [report PDF](https://avia.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/2024_Work_Trend_Index_Annual_Report.pdf); [The Decoder, "AI shame at work is a thing"](https://the-decoder.com/ai-shame-at-work-is-a-thing-according-to-a-microsoft-study/)
- **Slack Workforce Lab, Fall 2024 Workforce Index** (17,000+ desk workers, Nov 2024): 48% would be uncomfortable admitting to their manager that they used AI for at least one of 11 common tasks. Top reasons: it feels like cheating (47% of the uncomfortable), fear of looking less competent, fear of looking lazy. UK: 54%. Workers comfortable discussing AI with managers are 67% more likely to use it. — [Slack blog](https://slack.com/intl/zh-sg/blog/news/the-fall-2024-workforce-index-shows-executives-and-employees-investing-in-ai-but-uncertainty-holding-back-adoption); [TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/slack-workforce-study-generative-ai/); [Salesforce](https://www.salesforce.com/news/stories/ai-adoption-slows-statistics/)
- **WalkMe "AI in the Workplace" (Aug 2025; 1,000 US workers):** 48.8% have hidden AI use to avoid judgment, including 53.4% of C-suite leaders. Among Gen Z, 62.6% have passed off AI-assisted work as fully their own and 55.4% have feigned understanding of AI in meetings. Only 7.5% received extensive training. — [Fortune, Aug 29 2025, "What is AI shame?"](https://www.fortune.com/2025/08/29/what-is-ai-shame-readiness-gap-training-artificial-intelligence); [Allwork.space](https://allwork.space/2025/08/survey-finds-growing-ai-shame-phenomenon-readiness-gap-emerging-at-work/) (vendor survey)
- **Reif, Larrick & Soll (Duke), "Evidence of a social evaluation penalty for using AI," PNAS 122(19), May 2025:** 4 experiments, 4,400+ participants. People who use AI are rated lazier, less competent, less diligent and less independent than those getting equivalent non-AI help, and AI users *anticipate* this judgment. The penalty held across ages, occupations and genders, and reversed for tasks clearly suited to AI. — [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12088386/); [eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/news/ai-adoption-workplace-duke-study/); [Gigazine](https://www.gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20250802-using-ai-social-evaluation-penalty)
- Recipients are less likely to accept AI-generated apologies, judging them less genuine (Bar-Ilan University study, via coverage). — [InsideHook](https://www.insidehook.com/tech/chatgpt-ai-write-wedding-speech/amp) (secondary)

### Inferences
- Concealment is common at every level, from Gen Z (~62%) up to the C-suite (53%). The Closeted User may be one of the *largest* personas, not a niche. It also overlaps with the Wary Pragmatist: people who say "don't lie to me" may themselves hide their own use. That double standard is a strong rhetorical point for the speaker.
- The PNAS social penalty is direct evidence for "no negotiated etiquette." The social cost of disclosure is real, so people default to hiding.

### Gaps
- No study was found measuring concealment specifically among *creative professionals*, e.g., artists hiding AI use from peers or clients.
- No 2026 update to the Slack, Microsoft or KPMG concealment figures was found.

## 6. Published audience segmentations vs the speaker's six personas

### Takeaway
Published segmentations consistently find a large ambivalent or "evaluating" middle as the biggest group, plus a smaller enthusiast group and a smaller resistant group. That pattern matches the speaker's guess that the Wary Pragmatist is the largest. None of them has a distinct "Closeted User" or "Refuser on labor/ethics grounds" segment. Those two personas are the speaker's original contribution, and the evidence in §4–5 supports both.

### Cited Findings
- **Klaviyo AI persona research** (data Dec 2025, published Mar 2026; ~8,000 consumers in 8 countries): **Evaluators 43%, Enthusiasts 26%, Holdouts 21%, Skeptics 10%.** In this scheme Skeptics use AI regularly but double-check its output. Full trust is only 13% even though 60% use AI weekly. — [Klaviyo newsroom](https://www.klaviyo.com/newsroom/ai-persona-research); [Klaviyo trends](https://www.klaviyo.com/marketing-resources/ai-consumer-trends)
- **Statista "Decoding AI Consumers" (2026; US, UK, Germany):** Enthusiast, Skeptic, Avoider, AI-Assisted Shopper. The Skeptic group is described as the broad middle of the market rather than a resistant minority. — [Statista](https://www.statista.com/business/ai-consumers-whitepaper-report)
- **International Journal of Consumer Studies (2026; cluster analysis, n=391 North Americans):** Confident Individual Adopters, **Hesitant Pragmatists** (lower tech evaluations, higher anxiety, most distinct cluster), Socially-Driven Tech Enthusiasts. — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/ijcs.70219)
- **Electronic Markets (Springer; April 2023, n=344 ChatGPT early adopters):** AI Enthusiasts, Naïve Pragmatists, Cautious Adopters, Reserved Explorers. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12525-025-00853-0)
- **Pew's three-way item as an implicit segmentation (June 2026):** 52% more concerned, 37% equally excited and concerned, 9% more excited. — [Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2026/08/18/young-adults-in-the-us-are-increasingly-wary-of-ai-concerned-it-will-take-jobs/). Pew's painting item (49% like less / 48% no change) roughly maps onto Quality Skeptic/Refuser vs Indifferent. — [Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/09/17/from-political-speeches-to-songs-how-would-americans-react-if-they-found-out-ai-was-involved/)
- **Ipsos AI Monitor 2026:** excitement and nervousness are at near parity, "increasingly… the same people feeling both." This supports a large ambivalent middle. — [Ipsos](https://www.ipsos.com/en/global-attitudes-ai-2026-wonder-vs-worry-divide-deepens)
- **Deloitte 2025 Connected Consumer (~3,500 US):** 74% of those familiar with gen AI say its spread makes it harder to trust what they see online. — [Deloitte](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/insights/industry/telecommunications/connectivity-mobile-trends-survey.html)

### Inferences
Rough, illustrative mapping. These are *not* validated sizes:
- **Enthusiast** ≈ Pew's 9% "more excited" (US); Klaviyo's 26% Enthusiasts (global, consumer-commerce frame, so likely inflated).
- **Wary Pragmatist** ≈ Pew's 37% "equally both" + Klaviyo Evaluators 43% + the 71–89% who want labels. This is very plausibly the largest group, consistent with the speaker.
- **Refuser** ≈ Klaviyo Holdouts 21%, plus the strongly hostile shares in creative trades (GDC 52% negative; 85% of novelists expect income harm).
- **Quality Skeptic** has public backing: Merriam-Webster's "slop" and Pew's 53% "worse for creative thinking." But the Deezer data (§7) suggest quality alone doesn't drive the objection.
- **Indifferent** ≈ Pew's 48% "no change" on the AI-painting item, or the 65+ "not sure" group (29% unsure about personal impact).
- **Closeted User** has no counterpart in any published segmentation, yet 48–57% of workers conceal use (§5). It cuts across the other segments rather than being a separate one.

### Gaps
- No published segmentation was found that is specific to *creative-content audiences* (e.g., readers, gallery-goers, music fans) with sized segments.
- Klaviyo and Statista are commercial and shopping-framed, and their methodology was not reviewed.

## 7. How sentiment in creative communities changed 2023–2026; did better quality reduce or increase opposition?

### Takeaway
Opposition in creative and technical communities has *grown*, not shrunk, as the tools improved. GDC negativity nearly tripled (18%→52%), Stack Overflow favorability fell (72%→60%), Gen Z excitement fell (36%→22%), and "slop" became Merriam-Webster's 2025 word of the year. Better quality has made AI output *harder to detect* (97% fail a blind test) without lowering demand for disclosure (80% want labels). That suggests the objection is about authenticity, consent and labor, not only quality.

### Cited Findings
- GDC: negative view of gen AI's industry impact 18% (2024) → 30% (2025) → 52% (2026). Positive 21% → 13% → ~7%. — [Game Developer 2026](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/one-third-of-game-workers-use-generative-ai-but-half-think-it-s-bad-for-the-industry); [Game Developer 2025](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/gdc-2025-state-of-the-game-industry-devs-weigh-in-on-layoffs-ai-and-more)
- Stack Overflow: favorability 70%+ (2023), 72% (2024), 60% (2025). Trust in accuracy 43% → 33%. — [Stack Overflow 2025](https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2025/ai)
- Gallup Gen Z: excitement 36% → 22%, anger +9 to 31% (2025→2026). — [Gallup](https://news.gallup.com/poll/708224/gen-adoption-steady-skepticism-climbs.aspx)
- AOP photographers: the share losing work held at 58% (2025 vs 2026), but average losses rose 142%. — [PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2026/01/30/58-of-photographers-have-lost-work-to-generative-ai-survey)
- **Merriam-Webster 2025 Word of the Year: "slop"** (announced Dec 15, 2025), defined as "digital content of low quality that is produced usually in quantity by means of artificial intelligence." It was chosen partly on lookup volume, beating "gerrymander," "touch grass," "performative" and "tariff." — [TODAY](https://www.today.com/today/amp/rcna249280); [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/world/americas/merriam-webster-names-slop-2025-word-of-the-year-as-ai-content-explodes-1.500381158)
- **Deezer/Ipsos (fielded ~Oct 6–10, 2025; 9,000 respondents in 8 countries; published Nov 2025):** 97% failed to identify the fully AI-generated tracks in a blind test. 71% were surprised and 52% uncomfortable with that. ~80% want fully AI-generated music clearly labelled. — [Deezer newsroom](https://newsroom-deezer.com/2025/11/deezer-ipsos-survey-ai-music/); [Mixmag](https://mixmag.net/amp/97-percent-people-cant-tell-difference-between-ai-human-made-music-study-deezer) (the 80% figure is from secondary coverage)
- **BPI/AudienceNet "All About the Music 2025" (UK, 1,750+ music consumers; record-industry sponsor):** 81.5% want fully AI-generated music labelled. 78.5% say artists' music or vocals shouldn't be used by AI without permission. 82.7% say human creativity is essential to music. — [BPI](https://www.bpi.co.uk/news/2625735)
- Platform and institutional moves: Bandcamp banned music "generated wholly or in a substantial part" by AI and AI impersonation (statement dated Jan 13, 2025 per one source; another says late 2024; some coverage frames it as 2026). — [Electronic Groove](https://electronicgroove.com/bandcamp-bans-ai-generated-music-from-its-platform/); [Heise](https://heise.de/-11143045). San Diego Comic-Con's art show banned material "created by AI either partially or wholly" after artist pushback (Animation Magazine dates it Jan 2026; another source says Feb 2025). — [Animation Magazine](https://www.animationmagazine.net/2026/01/san-diego-comic-con-bans-ai-from-art-show); [Comics Beat](https://www.comicsbeat.com/sdcc-changes-art-show-policy-bans-ai-created-material)
- Pew: the public expects AI to *worsen* creative thinking (53% vs 16%), and young adults are the most likely to devalue AI art. — [Pew Sept 2025](https://www.pewresearch.org/science/2025/09/17/how-americans-view-ai-and-its-impact-on-people-and-society/)

### Inferences
- The "Quality Skeptic" stance may be partly a *proxy*. When listeners can't tell AI from human music (97%) yet still want labels (80%) and still say they'd like a painting less on learning it was AI (49%), the objection is to provenance, not to perceived quality. Better quality may *raise* opposition by increasing the deception risk. This fits the Wary Pragmatist's "don't lie to me."
- Rising usage alongside rising negativity (game devs, Gen Z, developers) supports the speaker's view that use and approval have decoupled.

### Gaps
- No longitudinal survey was found that tracks *the same* creative-professional panel's attitudes from 2023 to 2026, apart from GDC.
- No experimental study was found that directly tests whether improved AI quality increases or decreases acceptance.

## 8. Local / community-level backlash (2024–2026)

### Takeaway
There is a steady drumbeat of local controversies, mostly about AI-generated posters and flyers for fairs, festivals and arts events. The recurring grievance is that local artists were passed over for an event meant to celebrate local craft. Organizers usually say they used AI for convenience or to experiment, and often reverse course. These cases show the "no etiquette yet" claim playing out at small scale.

### Cited Findings
- **Oakland First Fridays (CA), April 2025:** community outrage over an AI-generated flyer, seen as contradicting the festival's mission of supporting local artists. The director said cost wasn't the reason. — [SF Chronicle](https://www.sfchronicle.com/entertainment/article/oakland-first-fridays-ai-art-20252866.php)
- **San Juan County Fair (WA), Jan–Feb 2025:** the fair board picked an AI poster as contest winner and drew immediate resident backlash. The artist (Paul Huber) defended AI as a tool and later withdrew the work. — [San Juan Journal, Feb 11 2025](https://www.sanjuanjournal.com/2025/02/11/sjc-fair-receives-backlash-for-ai-poster-artist-withdraws-work/)
- **Crowley Rice Festival (LA), 2025:** AI poster controversy. — [The Current (Lafayette)](https://thecurrentla.com/2025/rice-festival-poster-ai/)
- **Bozeman Christmas Stroll (MT), Nov 2025:** the winning poster was accused of being AI-made ("artgate"), and the Downtown Bozeman Partnership pulled it, citing safety concerns. — [Cowboy State Daily, Nov 29 2025](https://cowboystatedaily.com/2025/11/29/bozemans-christmas-poster-sparks-fierce-ai-art-backlash/)
- **Ohio State Fair, 2026:** an AI poster won first place ($1,000). Critics pointed out errors such as the wrong number of flag stars and stripes. An art-educator judge called for banning AI works from future state fair competitions. — [NBC4 Columbus](https://www.nbc4i.com/news/local-news/central-ohio-news/ai-poster-causes-controversy-in-ohio-state-fair-contest/)
- **Nova Scotia (Canada), 2026:** heritage advocates objected to AI posters promoting historic and tourist sites. A radio host began offering free design work to community groups as an alternative. — [CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/nova-scotia/backlash-against-ai-generated-graphics-grows-in-n-s-9.7308798)
- Data-center siting is a parallel local AI backlash: 69% oppose a local AI data center (NBC, Aug–Sept 2026), and only 1 in 9 support one (UMass, Sept 2026). — [UMass Poll](https://www.umass.edu/poll/about/reports/2026-09-national-public-opinion-poll-2)

### Inferences
- The pattern (organizer adopts AI quietly → local artists and residents object → reversal) shows community-level norms being negotiated *case by case*. That supports the speaker's "no negotiated etiquette" point.
- Fairs and festivals are flashpoints because their whole purpose is to celebrate local human craft.

### Gaps
- No systematic count or database of local AI-backlash incidents was found. The examples come from a limited search and are anecdotal.
- No cases were found involving local newspapers or newsletters (e.g., readers objecting to AI-written local news), apart from general news-reaction data in Pew (56% would react negatively to learning a news article was AI-written).
- No survey was found that directly measures etiquette norms, e.g., whether using AI for a wedding speech, condolence note or gift art is considered rude. Only anecdotes and the Bar-Ilan apology study exist.
